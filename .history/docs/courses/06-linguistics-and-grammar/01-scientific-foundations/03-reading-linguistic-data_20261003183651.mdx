## Three lines, different responsibilities

A linguistic example commonly has an original or segmented line, a
**morpheme-by-morpheme gloss**, and an idiomatic translation. A **morpheme**
is a minimal unit of grammatical or lexical meaning in an analysis. It need
not be a whole word, and its meaning need not correspond to a separate English word.

The following Latin example appears in the Leipzig Glossing Rules. Segmentation
and the explanation here are for teaching:

```text
insul-arum
island-GEN.PL
'of the islands'
```

`insul-` is the stem. `-arum` jointly expresses **genitive** (here, an "of"
relationship) and **plural**. `GEN.PL` uses a dot because multiple gloss
labels correspond to one segment, not because Latin has a hidden word for "of"
between two invisible suffixes. The free translation is idiomatic English, not
a second source-language transcript.

## Boundaries carry analytical commitments

| Notation | What it normally communicates |
| --- | --- |
| `root-affix` / `ROOT-LABEL` | A proposed segmentable morpheme boundary; hyphens correspond across lines |
| `word=clitic` | A clitic boundary; equals signs correspond across lines |
| `GEN.PL` | Several labels for one source element |
| `1SG`, `2PL`, `3SG` | First-person singular, second-person plural, third-person singular |
| `PST`, `PRS`, `NEG`, `Q` | Past, present, negation, question marker |
| `NOM`, `ACC`, `ERG`, `ABS` | Case labels, explained in the alignment lesson |

A **clitic** depends phonologically on a host but often behaves syntactically
more like a word than an ordinary affix. The distinction requires evidence,
not just spelling. A space in an orthography does not settle wordhood. Keep
the author's abbreviation key nearby: `S` may mean an intransitive argument,
not "singular," and category definitions vary between languages and theories.

## From rows to a paradigm

A **paradigm** organizes comparable forms by grammatical contrasts. Here is
a second **invented dataset**, with no claim to represent a real language:

| Form | Supplied meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `lum` | bird |
| `lum-ta` | birds |
| `lum-ki` | of a bird |
| `lum-ta-ki` | of birds |

The repeated contrasts support `-ta` as plural and `-ki` as genitive, with
plural before genitive in these forms. They do not show whether `lum-ki-ta`
is permitted, whether singular has a zero marker, or whether every noun follows
this pattern. A **zero** analysis posits a grammatical contribution without
overt material; it needs motivation from the broader system.

The key habit is to separate **form**, **contextual interpretation**, and
**analysis**. Glosses are part of an analysis, not neutral raw data. Two
researchers might agree on a recording yet disagree about its segmentation or
category labels. Preserve the original line and provenance so that disagreement
can be evaluated rather than hidden behind a tidy table.

## Try it yourself 🧪

Spend five minutes glossing `lum-ta-ki` and translating it idiomatically.
Then compare it with Latin `insul-arum`: both can express a genitive plural,
but where do the two categories live? Write one new noun contrast you would
request to test each segmentation. Expected constructed gloss: `bird-PL-GEN`.

<details>
<summary>🤓 What a transcription leaves out (optional)</summary>

Orthography, phonemic transcription, and narrow phonetic transcription serve
different purposes. Slash brackets such as `/p/` conventionally identify a
phonemic representation; square brackets such as `[pʰ]` represent phonetic
detail. Signed-language glosses such as `BOOK` are labels, not English words
being spoken by the signer. They omit handshape, movement, location, timing,
and nonmanual components unless those are separately annotated. A translation
cannot replace the original recording in either modality.

</details>

## Recap 🐾

1. Original forms, interlinear glosses, and free translations answer different questions.
2. Hyphens, equals signs, dots, and category labels express specific analytical choices.
3. Compare paradigms and preserve context before treating a gloss as a proven grammar.
