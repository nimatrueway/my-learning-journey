## Start with two independent dimensions

**Morphology** studies word structure. A **root** carries a central lexical
contribution; an **affix** is a bound element attached to a base. Prefixes
precede, suffixes follow, and infixes occur within a base. **Inflection**
typically supplies grammatical forms of a lexeme, such as number or tense.
**Derivation** typically creates a new lexeme, sometimes with a category
change: English *happy* to *happiness*. Borderline cases require language-specific evidence.

**Synthesis** asks how much morphological material is combined within words.
An **analytic** construction uses separate words for relevant grammatical
information; a **synthetic** one combines that information morphologically.
**Isolating** usually describes a tendency toward very little morphology and
roughly one morpheme per word. Analytic is broader: a separate auxiliary can
itself contain inflection.

**Exponence** asks how form maps to categories. In an **agglutinative** pattern,
separable pieces tend to correspond to individual grammatical functions. In a
**fusional** pattern, a piece can jointly express several categories. These
are tendencies, not mutually exclusive passports. A language can have high
synthesis and both agglutinative and fusional constructions.

## Tiny comparisons with real forms

The first two examples below illustrate familiar analyses; the Latin form
is also given in the Leipzig Glossing Rules. These are word forms, not full sentences.

```text
Turkish: ev-ler-de
         house-PL-LOC
         'in the houses'

Latin:   insul-arum
         island-GEN.PL
         'of the islands'

English: will walk / walk-ed
         separate auxiliary / walk-PST
```

Turkish `-ler` marks plural and `-de` locative, a location relationship.
Vowel harmony and other sound processes affect suffix forms: `-ler` is not
the only realization of Turkish plural. Latin `-arum` bundles genitive and
plural. English supplies both analytic and synthetic patterns within one
small comparison. Count morphologically justified pieces, not syllables or letters.

## Beyond a string of suffixes

Arabic has **root-and-pattern morphology**: consonantal roots participate
in templates containing vowels and other material. Indonesian uses reduplication
in several constructions; reduplication does not always mean a simple mandatory
plural. English *sing/sang* changes the stem; *go/went* is **suppletion**, where
a paradigm uses a markedly different stem. A word-based analysis may be more
useful than forcing every grammatical contrast into a detachable segment.

**Polysynthesis** refers to systems with particularly rich word-internal
grammatical organization, often including extensive argument indexing and,
in some languages, noun incorporation. The exact definition varies. Kalaallisut
(West Greenlandic) and Mohawk are important comparison cases, but a long word,
a compound, or any heavily suffixed form is not automatically polysynthetic.
**Noun incorporation** combines a noun stem into a larger predicate; it is
not simply deleting spaces in a written sentence.

go*. Say which contrasts you can segment and which you cannot. Finally,
explain why a character-count comparison of English and Kalaallisut would

<details>
<summary>🤓 Why typologists measure features, not pure types (optional)</summary>

Researchers can operationalize categories per word, morphological dependence,
and cumulative exponence separately. WALS chapter 22 measures **inflectional
synthesis of the verb**, not all words or all derivation. Its values depend
on coding criteria. Morphological richness is not total linguistic complexity:
syntactic, semantic, and phonological distinctions require different measurements.
None of these metrics ranks intelligence or sophistication of speakers.

</details>

## Recap 🐾

1. Synthesis measures packaging within words; exponence measures form–category mapping.
2. Analytic, agglutinative, fusional, and polysynthetic labels summarize tendencies with defined scope.
3. Stem changes, templates, suppletion, and reduplication prevent a suffix-only view of morphology.
