Two pronunciations can sound different yet count as the same word. One tiny
pitch change can also create a different word. A language does not use every
physically possible distinction: it organizes contrasts into a system.

## Phonetics describes; phonology organizes

**Phonetics** studies the production and perception of linguistic signals.
For speech, describe consonants by **place** of articulation, **manner**, and
**phonation**, including voicing. For example, `[p]` is a voiceless bilabial
stop: both lips close, airflow is blocked, then released, without vocal-fold
vibration during the closure in the canonical description.

Vowels can be described by tongue height, frontness/backness, and lip rounding.
The **International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)** supplies symbols for sounds,
not a universal spelling reform. The same letter can represent different
sounds across orthographies, and several letters can represent one sound.
Compare English `sh` with the IPA symbol `[ʃ]`.

**Phonology** studies language-specific contrasts and patterning. A **phoneme**
is a contrastive category in an analysis; an **allophone** is a realization
of that category. A **minimal pair** differs in one relevant sound in the
same environment and has different meanings. English *pat* and *bat* support
a `/p/` versus `/b/` contrast. The claim concerns a variety, not every accent
or every historical stage of English.

## Contrast versus predictable realization

In many English varieties, the `/p/` of *pin* is aspirated `[pʰ]`, while that
of *spin* lacks that aspiration. The raised `ʰ` means a following puff of air.
The distribution is predictable from context; aspiration does not normally
distinguish these English words from otherwise identical words.

In languages such as Hindi, aspiration can instead be contrastive. Do not
carry English's phoneme inventory into another language. **Complementary
distribution** (different sounds in different environments) supports an
allophone analysis when the sounds are phonetically related, but is not alone
sufficient proof: accidental gaps can also create nonoverlapping distributions.

## A world tour of phonological dimensions

| Dimension | Comparison worth investigating |
| --- | --- |
| Lexical tone | Mandarin, Yoruba, and Navajo use pitch contrasts despite different genealogies |
| Vowel or consonant length | Japanese distinguishes lengths that English spelling does not reliably represent |
| Click consonants | Found in several southern African families and also in languages such as Zulu through contact |
| Phonotactics | Languages differ in which consonant clusters and syllable patterns they permit |
| Signed parameters | Handshape, location, movement, and orientation can distinguish lexical signs |

**Tone** distinguishes words or grammatical forms; **intonation** contributes
utterance-level organization, such as prominence and some question patterns.
They can coexist. Mandarin citation forms `mā` "mother" and `mǎ` "horse"
illustrate a tonal contrast, but connected speech can change tone realization.
The diacritics are tone marks in pinyin, not English stress marks.

Signed languages have phonological organization without requiring sound.
Manual and nonmanual components, including facial actions, contribute to
linguistic structure. They are not merely emotional decoration. An English
gloss cannot show all those parameters, and one signed language's analysis
should not automatically be imposed on another.

## Try it yourself 🧪

Spend five minutes contrasting *pat/bat* and *pin/spin*. Write what changes,
what is held constant, and why the second pair is not itself a minimal pair
for aspiration: it also differs by `/s/`. Then explain what kind of controlled
comparison would establish a handshape contrast in a signed language without
using its English gloss as the evidence.

<summary>🤓 Categories are not waveform slices (optional)</summary>

Actual signals vary continuously across speakers and contexts. Coarticulation
means neighboring articulations overlap, so phones are analytical units, not
always cleanly separated acoustic chunks. Syllable, stress, tone, and timing
can require representations beyond a flat segment list. Signed languages may
organize simultaneous parameters differently from spoken languages; the exact
units and representations are questions for evidence and theory.

## Recap 🐾

1. Phonetics describes signals; phonology models a language's contrasts and distributions.
2. Minimal pairs and contextual alternations distinguish contrast from predictable realization.
3. Tone, length, phonotactics, and signed parameters are independent comparison dimensions.
