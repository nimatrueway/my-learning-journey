# 4.5 — Peeking inside: what did it learn to see? ⏱️ ~12 min

<Meme quote="Trust, but verify." author="Ronald Reagan — also applies to neural networks" />

## The hook

You trained a cat classifier. It scores 95%. But *what did it actually learn*? "The model works" and "the model learned what you think it learned" are very different claims — and the gap between them has embarrassed a lot of teams.

## The layer-by-layer x-ray 🩻

Researchers have visualized what each conv layer's kernels respond to, thousands of times, across many networks. The story is remarkably consistent:

- **Layer 1** — edges and color blobs. Almost identical in *every* vision network ever trained. (Remember the edge-detect kernel you slid in 4.1? Networks invent it on their own, every time.)
- **Layers 2–3** — combinations: corners, stripes, fur texture, honeycomb patterns
- **Middle layers** — object parts: eyes, ears, wheels, feathers
- **Late layers** — whole concepts: faces, cats, keyboards

Nobody asked for this hierarchy. It *emerges* from gradient descent, every time — pixels are best explained by edges, edges by parts, parts by objects.

## The cautionary tale: the husky that was snow 🐺

A famous study trained a wolf-vs-husky classifier. Great accuracy! Then researchers highlighted *which pixels* drove each decision…

The model had learned: **wolf = snow in the background.** Every training wolf photo had snow. The classifier was a snow detector with a wolf-shaped job title. On a husky-in-snow photo: "wolf."

<Meme top="my model: 95% accurate" visual="🐺❄️" bottom="also my model: a snow detector with delusions of grandeur" />

Simple tools that catch this:

- **Saliency maps** — highlight the input pixels that most influenced the verdict (one `backward()` call to the *input* — Module 2 blame, aimed at pixels!)
- **Grad-CAM** — a coarse heatmap of *where* the model looked
- **The eyeball test** — always inspect your model's most confident mistakes; that's where the weird lives

If the heatmap glows on the background, you don't have a cat classifier. You have a couch classifier that has met a lot of cats.

## Recap 📌

- CNN layers build an emergent hierarchy: edges → textures → parts → objects
- High accuracy ≠ right reasons — see: the snow-wolf incident
- Saliency/Grad-CAM = pointing blame at pixels; always audit *where* the model looks
