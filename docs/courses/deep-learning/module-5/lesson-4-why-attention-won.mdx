# 5.4 — Why attention won ⏱️ ~10 min

<Meme quote="The best way to have a good idea is to have a lot of ideas and throw the bad ones away." author="Linus Pauling — in 2017, \u2018recurrence\u2019 was the bad one" />

## The hook

For years, attention was a *side dish* — a helper bolted onto RNNs. Then in 2017, a paper asked the rudest question in ML history: what if we delete the RNN and keep **only** the attention?

The paper was titled *"Attention Is All You Need."* It was correct. Here's the scoreboard.

## The scoreboard 🏆

| | RNN / LSTM | Pure attention (Transformer) |
|---|---|---|
| Long-range links | fade over distance | full strength at any distance |
| Training | word-by-word, sequential 🐌 | all words at once ⚡ |
| GPU utilization | terrible (waiting in line) | glorious (one big matmul) |
| Scaling behavior | diminishing returns | keeps improving with size |
| Blame flow (backprop) | vanishes through steps | direct paths everywhere |

The sneaky-decisive row is **training speed**. Sequential processing meant RNNs couldn't efficiently use bigger GPU clusters — the hardware sat idle waiting for step *t-1*. Attention turned language into one giant parallel matrix party. Suddenly you could profitably throw 10,000 GPUs at text.

## Then came the scaling laws 📈

Once training was parallel, researchers tried making models bigger. And bigger. And the quality just… kept climbing — smoothly, predictably, following mathematical curves (**scaling laws**). No wall in sight.

That predictability changed AI from research into engineering: "10× the compute" became a *budgetable plan* with a forecastable result. GPT-1 (2018) → GPT-2 → GPT-3 (2020) → the chatbot you talked to today: mostly the same architecture, scaled along those curves.

<Meme top="2017: what if attention, but without the RNN" visual="📄💣" bottom="2023: every AI you've heard of is that paper" />

## The fine print (nothing is free)

- Attention is **O(n²)**: every word looks at every word, so 10× longer context = 100× cost. The reason "context length" is a marketing number.
- Attention has **no built-in sense of order** — "dog bites man" and "man bites dog" look identical to it. The fix (position embeddings) is part of the transformer, next module.

Both fine-print items are exactly what the transformer architecture handles — and that's Module 6.

## Recap 📌

- 2017, *Attention Is All You Need*: delete recurrence, keep attention — it won on every axis
- Parallelism was the kingmaker: it unlocked massive GPU scaling, then scaling laws made bigger = predictably better
- Costs accepted: O(n²) in length, and order must be added back manually
