# 5.3 — Attention: look at everything at once ⏱️ ~15 min

<Meme quote="The ability to observe without evaluating is the highest form of intelligence." author="Jiddu Krishnamurti — accidentally naming the attention mechanism" />

## The hook

The RNN's tragedy: by the time it reads "it," the "cat" has faded from memory.

The attention insight is almost insulting in its simplicity: **why remember, when you can look?** Keep every word available, and let each word *look back at all the others* whenever it needs to.

## Watch words look at each other 🎮

Every word computes how *relevant* every other word is to it, then blends their meanings — weighted by relevance. Click "it":

<AttentionViz />

**Things to try:**

1. Click **"it"** → 55% of its attention goes to **"cat"**. The pronoun resolves itself by *looking*, across any distance, at full strength. No fading. The RNN problem — deleted.
2. Click **"drank"** → attends to "cat" (who drank?) and "milk" (drank what?). The verb gathers its subject and object.
3. Click **"thirsty"** → looks at "cat" and "it". Adjectives hunt for their owners.
4. Click the boring words ("the") → mostly self-attention. Nothing to gather; carry on.

After one attention pass, each word's vector is no longer just its dictionary meaning — it's meaning **in context**. The vector for "it" now literally contains cat-ness. (Remember embedding-land's confused "mouse" from 5.1? Attention is what disambiguates it: nearby "cheese" vs nearby "USB".)

## How relevance is computed (the Q/K/V bit, humanely)

Each word plays three roles, produced by three small learned layers:

- **Query** — what I'm looking for ("I'm a pronoun; seeking: a recent noun, ideally furry")
- **Key** — how I advertise myself ("I'm a noun! An animal! Very concrete!")
- **Value** — what I actually hand over if you attend to me (my meaning)

Relevance = how well my query matches your key (a dot product — the same similarity math from 5.1!). Softmax the scores into percentages, then blend everyone's **values** by those percentages. That blend is the word's new, context-aware vector.

And the two RNN complaints? Fading memory: gone — every word attends to every other *directly*. No parallelism: gone — all words compute their attention **simultaneously**. One matrix multiply. GPUs weep with joy.

<details>
<summary>🤓 The actual math (optional — skip freely)</summary>

The most famous equation in modern AI:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$

- $QK^\top$ — every query dotted with every key: an $n \times n$ table of raw relevance scores
- $\sqrt{d_k}$ — a scale-down so the dot products don't grow huge and saturate softmax (lesson 1.4's flat zones strike again)
- $\text{softmax}$ — each row becomes percentages summing to 1 (the numbers you clicked through in the widget)
- $\cdot\,V$ — blend the value vectors by those percentages

Cost: the $n \times n$ table means attention is $O(n^2)$ in sequence length — why long-context LLMs are expensive, and why "efficient attention" is a research industry.

</details>

## Recap 📌

- Attention replaces *memory* with *direct lookup*: every word sees every word, no fading
- Query ("what I seek") × Key ("what I offer") → relevance %; blend Values accordingly
- Fully parallel — the property that made trillion-parameter models economically possible
