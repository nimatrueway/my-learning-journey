# 5.2 — RNNs: reading one word at a time ⏱️ ~10 min

<Meme quote="Those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat it." author="George Santayana — dedicating this one to RNNs" />

## The hook

Sentences have variable length, but networks want fixed-size inputs. The classic fix (pre-2017): read one word at a time, carrying a running **memory** — like you're doing with this very sentence.

That memory has a fatal flaw. Let's watch it fail.

## Read along 🎮

A **recurrent neural network** (RNN) keeps a fixed-size *hidden state* — its memory. Each new word gets blended in… and everything old fades a little. Feed the sentence and watch the bars:

<RNNViz />

**Things to try:**

1. Read up to "glass" → "cat" is already fading fast.
2. Read to the end → the sentence's *punchline* ("it was bored") needs to know **who** "it" is… and "cat" is nearly gone from memory. Who was bored? The glass? The table?
3. Reset and count: after just 5–6 words, the first word's signal is under 5%.

The blend-and-fade is the whole mechanism — and the whole problem. Each step *squeezes the entire past* into the same fixed-size box, and old information gets crushed to make room.

<Meme top="RNN reading a long paragraph" visual="🐱💭…💨" bottom="by sentence 3, the cat has left the memory" />

## Why it fades (and why training hurts too)

The forgetting curve isn't the only issue. Remember blame-flow from lesson 2.3? To learn "word 1 explains word 12," blame must flow backwards through *11 blend steps* — multiplied by a factor at each step. Multiply 11 numbers less than 1 → blame vanishes. Long-range learning starves. (Sound familiar? It's lesson 1.4's vanishing gradient, wearing a scarf.)

Band-aids were invented — **LSTM** and **GRU** add learnable "what to keep / what to forget" gates, and they genuinely help — but the fundamental squeeze remains: an entire book has to fit through a fixed-size pipe, one word at a time. Also: step 12 can't start until step 11 finishes. No parallelism. GPUs hate it.

Keep those two complaints in your pocket: **fading memory** and **no parallelism**. The next lesson dissolves both with one idea.

## Recap 📌

- RNN = read sequentially, blending each word into a fixed-size fading memory
- Long-range links fail twice: old info gets crushed, and blame vanishes through many steps
- LSTM/GRU = better forgetting, same squeeze; and sequential = slow on GPUs
