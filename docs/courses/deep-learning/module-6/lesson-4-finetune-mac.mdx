# 6.4 — Fine-tuning on your Mac ⏱️ ~12 min

<Meme quote="Do what you can, with what you have, where you are." author="Theodore Roosevelt — patron saint of laptop ML" />

## The hook

Full fine-tuning updates every knob — for a 7B-parameter model, the optimizer's bookkeeping alone wants ~50+ GB of memory. Your Mac politely declines.

Enter **LoRA** (Low-Rank Adaptation), the trick that made LLM fine-tuning a laptop activity — and the finale of this course's favorite theme: *freeze the expensive part, train a small cheap part*.

## The idea: sticky notes on a frozen model 📝

Module 4 transfer learning froze the network and replaced the head. LoRA is sneakier: it freezes **everything** and adds tiny *bypass adapters* alongside the big layers.

- Each big frozen weight layer gets a small parallel detour: input → tiny layer A → tiny layer B → added to the frozen layer's output
- The detour starts as a zero (no effect), and training shapes it into a *correction*
- The tiny layers are thin — the detour has ~0.1–1% as many knobs as the layer it corrects

Think of it as sticky notes on a textbook: the book (billions of knobs) stays pristine; your notes (millions) say "in *this* context, adjust the answer this way."

<details>
<summary>🤓 The actual math (optional — skip freely)</summary>

A frozen weight matrix $W$ (say $4096 \times 4096$ ≈ 16.8M knobs) gets a low-rank correction:

$$W' = W + BA$$

where $A$ is $r \times 4096$ and $B$ is $4096 \times r$ with rank $r$ tiny (4–64). At $r=8$: $BA$ is controlled by just $2 \times 8 \times 4096 \approx 65k$ knobs — **0.4%** of $W$ — yet can nudge the full matrix. $B$ starts at zero so training begins exactly at the pretrained behavior and drifts gently away.

The bet: fine-tuning *changes* are low-rank — adapting style/domain needs far fewer degrees of freedom than learning language did. Empirically, the bet pays off almost everywhere.

</details>

## What this buys you

- **Memory:** gradients + optimizer state only for ~0.5% of knobs → a small LLM fine-tunes in a few GB. MPS handles it.
- **Speed:** hours, not weeks
- **Portability:** the adapter file is megabytes. Share "my model" as a sticky-note pack; anyone applies it to the same base model.
- **Swappability:** one base model + N adapters = N specialized models on one disk

```python
# the entire idea, in PEFT-library form
from peft import LoraConfig, get_peft_model

model = get_peft_model(base_model, LoraConfig(r=8, lora_alpha=16))
model.print_trainable_parameters()
# trainable: 0.24% — your Mac just became an ML lab
```

The training loop after this? The sacred five lines. Of course it is.

## Recap 📌

- LoRA freezes the whole model and trains tiny bypass adapters (~0.5% of knobs)
- Starts at zero-effect, learns a low-rank *correction* — pretrained knowledge stays intact
- Result: LLM fine-tuning in a few GB of memory, on MPS, tonight — adapters are MB-sized and swappable
