# 6.5 — What's next: your map of the field ⏱️ ~10 min

<Meme quote="The journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step." author="Lao Tzu — you just took about thirty of them" />

## What you actually know now

Six modules ago, "deep learning" was a black box. Now trace the whole chain, no magic anywhere:

**Neurons** (multiply-add-squish) → **layers** → **loss** (one number for wrongness) → **gradient descent + backprop** (blame, distributed) → **training discipline** (data splits, overfitting, regularization) → **CNNs** (sliding pattern-detectors) → **transfer learning** (steal the eyes) → **embeddings** (meaning as coordinates) → **attention** (look, don't remember) → **transformers** → **tokenizers** → **pretraining/fine-tuning/LoRA**.

When a paper says "we fine-tuned a 7B decoder-only transformer with LoRA (r=16) on 50k instruction pairs" — that's not jargon anymore. That's a sentence you can *audit*.

## Roads from here 🗺️

Pick by pull, not by obligation:

- **Build things** (recommended first) — fine-tune models on your own data; build a semantic search with embeddings; enter a beginner Kaggle competition. Skills grow by shipping.
- **Go deeper on fundamentals** — Andrej Karpathy's *"Neural Networks: Zero to Hero"* videos: build GPT from scratch, by hand, in code. The perfect sequel to this course's intuition-first approach.
- **Read the classics** — you're ready for the real papers: *Attention Is All You Need* (2017), the ResNet paper (2015), *LoRA* (2021). Papers are just lessons with more Greek letters.
- **Specialize** — vision (segmentation, detection), audio (whisper-style models), RL (models that act), diffusion (image generation — the one big family this course didn't cover).
- **Engineering track** — serving models, quantization, evaluation pipelines: turning models into products. Scarce, valuable, very MSc-SE-shaped.

## The three habits that matter more than any topic

1. **Overfit one batch first** (lesson 3.5) — the debugging ritual that never stops paying
2. **Look at your data and your model's mistakes** (lessons 3.1, 4.5) — most "model problems" are data problems wearing a disguise
3. **Change one thing at a time** (lesson 3.4) — experiments, not vibes

<Meme top="day 1: what's a neuron?" visual="🐱🎓" bottom="today: ah yes, a low-rank adapter on the attention projections" />

One more thing: the final project awaits. Go fine-tune an actual language model on your own machine — the thing that, six modules ago, sounded like science fiction.

## Recap 📌

- You can trace pixels-or-text → prediction → loss → blame → learning, with zero magic steps
- Next moves: build things, Karpathy's Zero-to-Hero, read the original papers, or specialize
- The habits outlast the hype: overfit-one-batch, look at mistakes, change one thing at a time
