# 6.3 — Pretraining vs fine-tuning: how LLMs are raised ⏱️ ~12 min

<Meme quote="Education is what remains after one has forgotten what one has learned in school." author="Einstein — pretraining in one sentence" />

## The hook

A freshly pretrained GPT is *not* a chatbot. Ask it "What's the capital of France?" and it might reply: `What's the capital of Spain? What's the capital of Italy?` — because on the internet, questions travel in lists. It's a magnificent autocomplete with no idea it's supposed to *answer* you.

Turning that raw text-predictor into a helpful assistant is a multi-stage upbringing.

## Stage 1: Pretraining — reading the internet 📚

- **Task:** predict the next token. That's all. Your mini-project 5, at cosmic scale.
- **Data:** trillions of tokens — web pages, books, code
- **Cost:** thousands of GPUs, weeks-to-months, tens of millions of dollars
- **Result:** a **base model** — vast knowledge of language, facts, code, and reasoning patterns… and zero manners

Why does "predict the next token" produce knowledge at all? Because predicting well *requires* it. To continue `The capital of France is`, you must know Paris. To continue a Python function, you must know Python. Compressed into those knobs is everything that helps guess the next word — which turns out to be almost everything.

## Stage 2: Fine-tuning — teaching manners 🎓

Same training loop, same cross-entropy, but now the data is tens of thousands of curated *conversations*: instruction → good response. The model learns the **shape of being helpful**: answer the question, format nicely, decline the sketchy stuff.

This is Module 4's transfer learning, verbatim: expensive general pretraining (the frozen "eyes"), cheap task-specific adaptation (the new "head"). Same idea, new domain.

**Stage 3 (the polish):** after fine-tuning, models are further tuned on *human preferences* — people rank candidate answers, and the model is nudged toward the ranked-higher kind (RLHF and friends). That's the difference between "technically correct" and "actually pleasant."

## Why this matters to *you*

The stages have wildly different price tags:

| Stage | Cost | Who can do it |
|---|---|---|
| Pretraining | $10M+ | a handful of companies |
| Full fine-tuning | hundreds–thousands of $ | funded teams |
| **LoRA fine-tuning** | **your Mac, tonight** | **you, next lesson** |

You never start from scratch. You take a pretrained model and adapt it — and the cheap way to adapt is where we go next.

## Recap 📌

- Pretraining = next-token prediction on trillions of tokens → knowledge without manners
- Fine-tuning = same loop on curated conversations → the shape of helpfulness (transfer learning!)
- Preference tuning = the final polish from human rankings; adaptation is cheap, pretraining is not
