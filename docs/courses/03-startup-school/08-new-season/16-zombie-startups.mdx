# Zombie Startups: Should They Shut Down or Keep Going?

Payroll clears. Customers remain. The company is not dying, but it is no longer
going anywhere. Planning expands because the product and growth conversations
have stopped producing energy.

Survival can conceal the need for a decision.

⏱️ **~6 min** · includes 2 minutes of practice · the 18-minute source video is optional

## Name the State Before Choosing the Exit

The hosts call a company a “zombie startup” when it has money, revenue, and
customers but little meaningful growth. The label is intentionally informal.
Its diagnostic value is separating temporary difficulty from indefinite drift:
the team repeats familiar tactics, bureaucracy grows, and everyone waits for an
external event to restore momentum.

Honest diagnosis creates three broad paths: shut down, pursue acquisition, or
continue with a materially different direction. “Keep doing the same thing” is
not a fourth strategy. It is the default that produced the current state.

**Source-reading note:** the captions distinguish turns without reliably naming
each speaker, so uncertain office-hour stories remain attributed to “one host.”
The zombie label, suggested timelines, and radical-action advice are speaker
heuristics, not legal, financial, or fiduciary guidance.

## Shutdown Can Honor the Obligation

One host describes founders feeling they have failed employees and investors.
His reframing is that the obligation was to try hard, intelligently, and
ethically, not to preserve one company forever. Investors hold portfolios.
Employees may benefit more from freedom to pursue growing work than from being
kept aboard a stalled company.

The source also recounts an unnamed company that shut down roughly four years
earlier; team members went on to strong roles, including work at AI labs. This
is an anecdote, not proof that every shutdown improves every career. It does
show why “continuing protects the team” deserves examination rather than
automatic acceptance.

Mechanically, the hosts recommend clarity and a date. A vague hope of being
acquired can prolong paralysis. A bounded process might allow two months for
introductions and conversations, followed by shutdown if no deal appears.
Acquisitions are less frequent and less lucrative than press coverage implies,
especially when the potential buyer has no existing relationship with the team.

## Try It Yourself: Write a 30-Day Decision Contract

Take two minutes and complete five lines:

1. We are stalled because **___** has not improved for **___** months.
2. By day 30, turnaround evidence means **___**.
3. The uncomfortable action we will test is **___**.
4. We will stop the test early if **___**.
5. If the evidence is absent, we will **shut down / begin a dated acquisition process / choose another explicit path**.

Share the contract with the cofounders and one informed outsider. A deadline
that only exists in your head is remarkably open to renegotiation.

## Continuing Requires Discontinuous Action

False escape paths preserve comfort: hire a supposed growth savior, pour more
leads into a leaky product, or fundraise on a fashionable AI story. Those moves
add resources without resolving why customers do not stay, pay, or expand.

The hosts' stronger heuristic is to try something the team would not normally
do precisely because normal tactics have failed. That could mean narrowing to a
painful customer segment, changing pricing, replacing a cherished workflow, or
putting founders directly into sales. Radical does not mean reckless. The test
still needs a hypothesis, ethical boundary, runway limit, and measurable result.

Profitability is an important countercondition. A slow-growth company that
supports its owners and employees may be a healthy small business if everyone
chooses that objective honestly. It becomes a zombie through mismatch: venture-
scale promises and opportunity costs persist while the operating reality no
longer supports them.

<details>
<summary>🧟 Acquisition reality check (optional)</summary>

List plausible buyers with whom the company already has a relationship, the
specific asset or team capability each would value, and the person who can make
an introduction. Do not assume a small revenue stream matters to a billion-
dollar buyer or that a press release means founders became wealthy. Set a date
after which the orderly shutdown path begins.

</details>

## Three Things to Keep

- A zombie startup survives operationally while growth, energy, and learning remain stalled.
- Shutdown can fulfill obligations better than trapping employees and founders in indefinite drift.
- Continuing is credible only with a bounded, materially different test and a decision date.
