# Is My Startup Growing Fast Enough?

Your enterprise product closes one customer after a six-month sales cycle. Then
you see a self-serve AI company announcing explosive growth and wonder whether
your company is already dead.

⏱️ **~7 min** · includes 2 minutes of practice · the 12-minute source video is optional

Dalton Caldwell and Michael Seibel argue that unusually fast AI growth is real.
They also argue that a famous company's number can be the wrong ruler. Their
advice is an investor-and-founder heuristic: compare like with like, then inspect
the quality beneath the rate.

## A Growth Number Has a Denominator and a Story

“We grew 6x” is incomplete. Six times which measure, from what starting value,
over what period, with what retention and cost? A tiny base can multiply quickly.
A one-off pilot can look recurring when annualized. Usage revenue can disappear
the moment a customer changes providers.

Before reacting to a benchmark, write five fields:

1. **Metric:** cash collected, contracted revenue, active users, or retained usage.
2. **Window:** weekly, monthly, or yearly.
3. **Base:** the starting amount, not only the multiple.
4. **Quality:** recurrence, gross margin, retention, and concentration.
5. **Comparable:** companies with a similar customer, product, and sales motion.

This does not make slower growth acceptable by default. It makes the diagnosis
specific enough to support action.

## Source Case: Cursor Is Not Every Company's Clock

Michael describes meeting founders who considered pivoting because they were not
growing as quickly as Cursor, even when they had the strongest product in a
different category. The comparison ignored basic mechanics. A self-serve tool
can acquire and activate customers at a speed unavailable to enterprise software
that requires security review, procurement, and implementation.

Dalton's stated investor heuristic is relative comparison. For example, a
vertical voice-AI company raising a Series A will be compared with other
companies addressing similar buyers and workflows. Even then, he says he would
inspect whether contracts are real and whether the product is good rather than
rank companies by a naked multiple.

The source also warns that reported annual recurring revenue can be fuzzy. A
company might multiply one month's usage by twelve, count a nonbinding letter of
intent, display a pilot customer's logo as if it were a durable deployment, or
resell model usage at negative margin. Those figures are not economically
equivalent to retained, profitable recurring revenue.

## Try It Yourself: Build One Honest Benchmark

Take your primary growth claim and spend 15 minutes rewriting it as a complete
sentence: “From **date** to **date**, **metric** moved from **base** to **result**;
**retention**, **gross margin**, and **customer concentration** are **values**.”

Then name three comparables with the same buyer and sales motion. If you cannot
find three, use your own last four periods instead. Pick one next action that
would improve the weakest underlying driver during the next two weeks. Do not
change the company idea during this exercise.

## Expect Surprise Without Excusing Stagnation

The hosts recall that Stripe and Teespring once looked similarly promising to
people close to both. They also name earlier apparent winners such as Yahoo,
Palm, AOL, and Netscape whose positions did not endure. Dalton explicitly says
he does not know how sticky Cursor or competing coding tools will prove to be.
That uncertainty matters in an early platform shift.

It does not follow that founders should celebrate weak growth. The source's
balanced move is to use faster companies as evidence that new tools may enable
more customer value, while refusing to treat one outlier as a universal shutdown
threshold. A multi-year company that is genuinely flat may need a serious pivot;
the episode mentions Standard applicants whose well-considered AI pivots changed
their trajectory. Context changes the prescription.

<details>
<summary>📏 Growth-quality questions (optional)</summary>

For a revenue metric, ask:

- Did a customer pay, sign, pilot, or merely express interest?
- Can the customer stop next month, and how often do customers actually do so?
- Does serving more usage produce positive gross profit?
- Is growth broad, or does one account dominate it?
- Would the same claim still sound strong without annualizing it?

These questions do not produce one perfect metric. They expose which assumptions
must hold for the headline to become durable company value.

</details>

## Three Things to Keep

- A growth multiple is interpretable only with its metric, period, base, and economic quality.
- Compare your company with businesses sharing its buyer and sales motion, not the loudest outlier online.
- Early markets reward speed but remain unpredictable; use benchmarks to improve, not to manufacture certainty.
