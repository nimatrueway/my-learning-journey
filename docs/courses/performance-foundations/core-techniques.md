# Core Techniques

⏱️ **~5 min** · reference — read once, revisit often

Four techniques keep reappearing across this course because the same brain
runs all of these problems. This page is their canonical home: lessons give a
one-line refresher and link here, and here is where the mechanics and the
science live.

<Meme quote="Give me six hours to chop down a tree and I will spend the first four sharpening the axe." author="attributed to Abraham Lincoln — this page is the axe" />

## Defusion

**What it is:** naming a thought or feeling as a mental event instead of
obeying it as a fact.

**The move:** add an observing phrase in front of the experience —

- “This is boredom.” (during effortful work)
- “I am noticing the thought that my work does not matter.” (before speaking up)
- “I am experiencing the *feeling* of inadequacy; the *fact* is my work met the
  spec.” (when the imposter narrative spikes)

Defusion does not argue with the thought or prove it false. It converts a
verdict into an observation, which restores the gap where you choose the next
action.

**Used in:** [Part I](/courses/performance-foundations/part-1-strengthening-focus)
(labeling discomfort), [Part IV](/courses/performance-foundations/part-4-speaking-with-confidence)
(distance from social predictions), [Part VII](/courses/performance-foundations/part-7-imposter-syndrome)
(feelings vs. facts).

<details>
<summary>🧠 The science (optional)</summary>

*Affect labeling* — putting feelings into words — reduces amygdala reactivity
while increasing right ventrolateral prefrontal activity (Lieberman et al.,
2007), dampening the emotional signal that drives escape or avoidance. In
Acceptance and Commitment Therapy the same move is called *cognitive defusion*:
research supports it as a process that reduces the believability and distress
of thoughts, while precise neural mechanisms remain an active research area.

</details>

## Evidence File

**What it is:** one running document of objective facts about your work,
maintained so that impressions cannot quietly rewrite the record. One file —
not a separate journal per lesson.

**The move:**

1. Shortly after finishing work (or a meeting, or receiving feedback), write
   what objectively happened: deliverables shipped, requests fulfilled,
   decisions influenced, errors found and fixed.
2. Strip subjective ratings — no “good,” “bad,” or “barely acceptable.”
3. When an impression arrives (“this was useless,” “I contributed nothing,”
   “I'm a fraud”), write it next to the facts and let them disagree in public.

| Impression | Objective facts |
| --- | --- |
| “This report is useless and nobody cares.” | Requested by leadership; used in planning; zero errors found. |
| “I don't deserve this salary.” | Deliverables used, approved, and compensated for two years. |

**Used in:** [Part IV](/courses/performance-foundations/part-4-speaking-with-confidence)
(fact vs. impression after meetings), [Part VI](/courses/performance-foundations/part-6-letting-success-count)
(logging wins before the auditor discounts them), [Part VII](/courses/performance-foundations/part-7-imposter-syndrome)
(reading the file before arguing with the fraud narrative).

<details>
<summary>🧠 The science (optional)</summary>

This is *cognitive restructuring* plus a defense against *confirmation bias*:
low self-worth filters attention toward mistakes and discards evidence of
competence, so the correction must be external and written. Documented facts
force a disciplined comparison between automatic interpretations and evidence —
the mechanism is the comparison, not forced positivity.

</details>

## Micro-Exposure

**What it is:** deliberately entering a feared or resisted situation at the
smallest useful size, while dropping the protective rituals that prevent
learning.

**The move:** shrink until starting is nearly trivial, then start —

- A deliberately rough 15-minute draft (creative fear, [Part II](/courses/performance-foundations/part-2-acting-despite-fear))
- Putting on work shoes as the entire commitment (physical inertia, [Part III](/courses/performance-foundations/part-3-overcoming-physical-inertia))
- One contribution in the first five minutes of a meeting (social fear, [Part IV](/courses/performance-foundations/part-4-speaking-with-confidence))

The rep only counts clean: no disclaimers, no apologies, no waiting to feel
ready. Grade the experiment by whether you did it, never by whether anxiety
vanished.

<details>
<summary>🧠 The science (optional)</summary>

Exposure research — particularly *inhibitory learning theory* — indicates the
goal is new safety learning (“I acted while anxious and the catastrophe did not
occur”), not fear elimination. Graded exposure starting at low intensity is
more sustainable than all-or-nothing plunges, and removing *safety behaviors*
matters because they let the brain credit survival to the ritual instead of to
your competence.

</details>

## Prediction Hedging

**What it is:** the brain's habit of dampening present experience to insure
against future feelings — one mechanism wearing several costumes:

- **Bracing:** “expect the worst, then reality can't hurt you” ([Part V](/courses/performance-foundations/part-5-expecting-the-worst))
- **Foreboding joy:** downplaying a win so its loss won't sting ([Part VI](/courses/performance-foundations/part-6-letting-success-count))
- **The fraud narrative:** “I never deserved it” as a pre-written explanation
  for future failure ([Part VII](/courses/performance-foundations/part-7-imposter-syndrome))

**The counter-move:** notice the insurance purchase, then check the premium
against the payout. The dread, dampened joy, and self-discounting are paid
daily and with certainty; the promised cushioning against future disappointment
is mostly illusory. Full mechanics and the recalibration protocol live in
[Part V](/courses/performance-foundations/part-5-expecting-the-worst).

## How to Use This Page

- Read it once early, then let the lessons send you back as needed.
- Keep exactly one Evidence File.
- When stuck, the order of operations is usually: defuse the thought, check
  the file, shrink the next step, decline the insurance.
