# What Does It REALLY Mean To Do Things That Don't Scale?

Doing things that do not scale is not permission to run a permanently manual
business. It is permission to use founder effort where software, process, and
reputation do not exist yet. The effort should create users, knowledge, or both.

⏱️ **~7 min** · includes 2 minutes of practice · the 19-minute source video is optional

## The Manual Step Has Two Jobs

Before product-market fit, a startup usually lacks enough volume to justify a
system and enough knowledge to design the right one. Manual work addresses both
problems. A founder can recruit the first users one at a time, deliver an outcome
with improvised operations, and observe where value or friction actually occurs.

The phrase is often misread as celebrating toil. The useful distinction is
between **temporary learning work** and **hidden permanent labor**. Temporary
work has a question and an exit condition. Hidden labor props up unit economics
or quality that the eventual product cannot reproduce.

For every manual step, record the user outcome it enables, the uncertainty it
resolves, the pattern it reveals, and the signal that justifies automation,
delegation, or stopping. If founders onboard ten customers and learn that every
team imports the same spreadsheet, they found a product requirement. If each
customer needs unrelated weekly consulting forever, they may have found a
service business rather than a scalable product.

## Source Case: Reddit Had to Create Its Own Motion

The episode describes early Reddit as more than launching a website and waiting.
The founders created initial activity, introduced subreddits, ran ads to recruit
people into particular communities, and moderated those communities. A mature
Reddit could rely on users and moderators; an empty Reddit could not. The manual
work supplied the social proof that the product itself required.

This case explains why “we launched” is not a distribution strategy. A network
product may need founders to manufacture the first useful network. The relevant
question is whether real users begin contributing and returning once they see
the value, not whether founder-created activity makes a screenshot look busy.

## Source Case: DoorDash Founders Owned the Failure

Caldwell and Seibel also discuss DoorDash before specialized teams and mature
operations. The founders handled deliveries and customer support themselves.
When an order went wrong, there was no department to blame; they apologized and
fixed it. That proximity exposed restaurant delays, driver problems, confusing
status information, and the emotional cost of a missing meal.

The work was operationally ugly but informationally rich. It taught the founders
which failures demanded software and which demanded a process. Delegating support
too early would have converted those lessons into a weekly report.

Cruise supplies a product version of the same idea. Seibel recalls an early test
ride whose capability was far narrower than the mature autonomous-driving
vision. The first version did not need to solve every road. It needed to make a
specific capability real enough to learn what came next.

## Original Example: The Nightly Reconciliation Desk

A startup helps independent pharmacies compare wholesaler invoices. Instead of
building integrations for every wholesaler, the founders ask five pharmacies to
upload PDFs. Each night they manually normalize line items and return a savings
report by morning.

After three weeks, 80 percent of discrepancies come from four fee formats. That
structure justifies parsers for those formats. They also discover pharmacists
will not pay for a report without links to dispute each charge, so the next
feature is a dispute workflow, not another integration. Manual delivery bought
specific product knowledge.

## Try It Yourself: Run a Founder Service Shift

Choose one onboarding, delivery, or support step that founders rarely observe.
Handle it personally for three users this week. For each session, write the
trigger, every action taken, elapsed minutes, user language, and final outcome.

At the end, mark each action as eliminate, explain, automate, or keep human. Pick
one automation candidate. It must repeat across at least two sessions and
materially improve the user's outcome or delivery cost. Set a threshold, such
as 20 weekly cases, for building it. The exercise succeeds even if the answer is
“do not scale this.” An impossible manual service is valuable evidence too.

<details>
<summary>🧭 Manual heroics can lie (optional)</summary>

Founder intensity can create a level of speed, care, or customization that a
future organization cannot afford. Track labor and failure rates from day one.
Do not promise regulated, safety-critical, or time-sensitive outcomes through an
unmonitored manual process. The shortcut should expose the mechanism, not hide
an impossible cost structure or transfer risk to users.

</details>

## Three Things to Keep

- Manual founder work is valuable when it creates an early user outcome and resolves a named uncertainty.
- Reddit, DoorDash, and Cruise used narrow, hands-on beginnings to reveal the community, operations, or capability their later systems needed.
- Measure repetition and labor, then automate only the pattern that demand and learning have made clear.
