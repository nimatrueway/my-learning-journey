# The Cult of Conformity in Silicon Valley

Once rebellion has a dress code, following it is not independence. A founder can
reject the conventional career ladder while obediently climbing a startup-shaped
version of the same ladder.

⏱️ **~7 min** · includes 2 minutes of practice · the 18-minute source video is optional

## When an Outsider Field Becomes a Status Path

Caldwell and Seibel compare tech in the early 2000s with the industry two decades
later. Seibel recalls that Yale graduated only ten computer-science majors in his
class of roughly 1,200. Caldwell describes Stanford just after the dot-com crash,
when choosing a startup meant entering visible wreckage. Finance, consulting,
law, and medicine offered clearer applications, recruiters, steps, and prestige.
Startups mostly attracted people who liked computers, disliked waiting for
permission, and wanted to make things immediately.

Success changed the selection pressure. Once software companies produced wealth
and recognizable founders, tech began attracting some of the same people who had
optimized for the approved school and approved profession. The new script could
be: study computer science, join the famous company, start the fashionable kind
of startup, enter the accelerator, and raise from the expected investors.

None of those choices is inherently wrong. The danger is choosing them because
the sequence is legible to peers rather than because it helps a particular user.

## Status Creates a Hidden Objective Function

A company can claim to optimize for customers while quietly optimizing for being
recognizable as a startup. The hidden objective appears in choices such as a
prestigious investor who is a poor partner, a fashionable market the team does
not understand, or headcount hired to make the company look established.

Run a simple counterfactual: if nobody impressive could observe this decision,
would you still make it? Then ask a second question: what customer or operating
evidence supports it? The first detects signaling; the second prevents
nonconformity from becoming its own empty pose.

Independent judgment is not reflexive opposition. A founder who rejects every
standard practice is still controlled by the crowd, only with the sign reversed.
The useful skill is selecting evidence close to the work and accepting a boring
answer when it fits.

## The Line Is Comfortable

The episode contrasts founders who wanted to do things now with careers that
required another ten or twenty years of progression. A line supplies reassurance:
complete this credential, earn this title, then receive permission for larger
work. Startups do not supply that structure. There is no grader who can certify
that the market will care.

Conformity therefore returns under uncertainty. Founders copy pitch language,
company rituals, or product categories because imitation reduces the emotional
cost of choosing. But a copied choice can only transfer when the underlying
constraints transfer too. The famous company’s office policy, pricing, or org
chart may solve a scale problem you do not have.

## Original Example: The Enterprise Costume

A two-person security startup has three design partners. One founder proposes a
vice president of sales, an annual conference sponsorship, and a downtown office
because enterprise companies must look serious. The other writes what each item
would change in the next eight weeks. None improves the security review currently
blocking all three pilots.

They spend a fraction of the budget on an independent assessment and founder-led
implementation calls. That choice looks less like the startup script but directly
attacks the constraint. It is independent because it follows evidence, not
because it is unusual.

## Try It Yourself: Remove One Audience

List three current decisions that an admired founder, investor, or peer could
notice. For each, write the practical outcome, the status audience, and the
evidence you would require if the audience disappeared. Pick one decision with
weak practical evidence. Delay it for seven days and redirect the same time or
money toward the closest customer constraint. Record what changed.

<details>
<summary>🧭 Conventions often contain compressed experience (optional)</summary>

Security reviews, accounting controls, legal documents, and hiring references
are not costumes merely because other companies use them. Some conventions
encode failures discovered repeatedly. Ask what failure a practice prevents and
whether that failure exists at your stage before discarding it.

</details>

## Three Things to Keep

- Tech can reproduce the credential ladder it once allowed outsiders to bypass.
- A status choice becomes visible when removing the audience removes the reason for making it.
- Independent judgment follows nearby evidence, even when the resulting decision is conventional or boring.
