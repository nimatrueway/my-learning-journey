# Should You Quit Your Job At A Unicorn?

"Unicorn" reports a private valuation at one moment. It does not tell an
employee whether customers stay, revenue grows, options have value, or the role
still compounds skills. Decide from inside evidence, not the logo.

⏱️ **~7 min** · includes 2 minutes of practice · the 14-minute source video is optional

## Employees Can See What Headlines Cannot

The episode begins from roughly 1,400 private companies carrying unicorn
valuations. They cannot all produce equally successful public offerings. Some
will become exceptional companies; some will sell below their last valuation;
others will shrink or fail.

Employees often possess better evidence than commentators and sometimes better
evidence than investors. They can see whether customers renew, revenue grows,
roadmaps stabilize, strong colleagues stay, and leaders answer hard questions.
The title attached to the company is weaker than these operating signals.

Start with product-market fit. Revenue alone is incomplete, but growing revenue,
retention, pricing power, and enthusiastic customer behavior form a coherent
case. If management withholds every useful metric, the opacity itself belongs
in the decision.

## Understand the Option, Not the Headline Value

Late-stage employees often receive options with a strike price connected to a
high private valuation. If the company later sells below that level or reprices
downward, those options may be underwater. Vesting more units does not help if
the spread between eventual share value and exercise price is zero.

An acquisition also does not guarantee a clean win. Employees may need to
reinterview, may not be retained, or may land inside the large technology
company they originally avoided. Treat equity as a risky concentrated asset,
not deferred salary at the last fundraising price.

Build scenarios: strong IPO, ordinary acquisition, down round, and failure.
Include exercise cost, taxes requiring professional advice, vesting, and the
value of salary and learning you give up by staying.

## Staying Can Be the Bold Choice

The hosts explicitly preserve the positive case. If metrics are excellent,
founders remain focused, colleagues are impressive, and your responsibility is
growing, staying may be unusually valuable. They compare that pattern with the
early trajectories of Google and Facebook, where long-tenured employees learned
inside companies that continued to compound.

Do not quit merely to perform courage. Leave when the company evidence weakens,
the role stops teaching, trust erodes, or a tested alternative offers a better
trajectory. A fantasy of quitting is information, but it becomes a decision only
after examining finances and the destination.

## Original Example: The Payments Platform

An engineer at a $2 billion payments startup sees flat merchant retention,
frequent strategy resets, and option exercise cost equal to a year of savings.
Her team now maintains internal approvals rather than the payment engine she
joined to build. Meanwhile, she has six months of runway and three paid pilots
for a reconciliation tool.

The alternative is not risk-free, but it is specific. She asks finance for the
option details, models a down-round outcome, completes another pilot, and sets a
departure date. Another engineer on the core fraud team, with expanding scope
and strong customer metrics, could rationally stay at the same company.

## Try It Yourself: Write a Stay Case and a Leave Case

Create two columns and force both to be credible:

1. Record revenue direction, retention, customer pull, and transparency.
2. Model your options at four plausible exit prices.
3. Score current learning, ownership, colleagues, and energy.
4. Describe the alternative using evidence, runway, and next milestone.
5. Set one event or date for a fresh decision.

If the leave column contains only frustration, test an alternative. If the stay
column contains only unvested option count, inspect what those options require
to become valuable.

<details>
<summary>🧭 Private-company information is incomplete (optional)</summary>

Employees rarely know every preference stack, financing term, or acquisition
scenario. Option and tax decisions may require qualified advice. Use ranges,
ask direct questions, and avoid treating either internal optimism or external
gossip as certainty.

</details>

## Three Things to Keep

- A unicorn valuation is weaker evidence than customer retention, revenue quality, leadership focus, and transparency.
- Late-stage options can be underwater, so model strike price and exit scenarios instead of counting units.
- Compare the compounding value of the current role with a tested alternative; do not leave or stay for status.
