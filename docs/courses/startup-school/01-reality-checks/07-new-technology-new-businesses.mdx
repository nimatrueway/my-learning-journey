# How New Technology Creates New Businesses

When a powerful technology arrives, the first business idea is usually an old
product with a new feature. The larger opportunity appears when the technology
changes who can produce value, what it costs, or how customers behave.

Platform shifts create new verbs, not just faster nouns.

⏱️ **~8 min** · includes 2 minutes of practice · the 8-minute source video is optional

## Start With the Constraint That Disappeared

Caldwell and Seibel describe several waves they watched firsthand. Early web
builders could earn money making basic sites because few people knew the tools.
Online marketplaces let individuals buy locally and sell to a much larger
audience. Open-source software allowed the Justin.tv team to assemble a product
without licensing every technical layer. Cloud computing later let a small team
rent a server with a credit card instead of buying and racking hardware.

Each wave removed a different constraint:

- the web reduced the cost of global publication;
- marketplaces reduced the cost of reaching buyers;
- open source reduced the cost of software building blocks;
- cloud services reduced the upfront cost of computing;
- app stores reduced distribution friction for mobile software.

The opportunity is not "use the new technology." It is a previously uneconomic
activity that becomes affordable, accessible, or convenient because of it.

## Map the Leverage to a New Producer

Technology shifts often let a smaller producer do work that once required an
institution. Before online marketplaces, a merchant might need a storefront.
Before home-sharing platforms, earning from spare lodging meant operating a bed
and breakfast or owning a hotel. A platform can turn an existing room, skill,
audience, or collection into a small business.

AI may create a similar reduction in required headcount. A single operator can
draft, classify, translate, analyze, or support at a volume that once required a
team. That does not automatically produce a defensible company. It does broaden
the set of people who can test one.

Ask two linked questions:

1. Who can now produce an outcome they could not afford before?
2. What will that person repeatedly do with the new leverage?

The second question filters impressive demos that support no habit or transaction.

## Whatnot: Learn the Format Before It Looks Normal

The episode uses Whatnot, a live-shopping platform, to show how early platform
literacy becomes economic advantage. Caldwell describes high-school-aged sellers
who learned the live format, built an audience, and earned meaningful money by
selling through it. They did not invent video streaming, payments, or online
marketplaces. They understood the combination before most sellers did.

The opportunity existed at more than one scale. Whatnot could grow into a large
company while an individual seller built a livelihood on it. Twitch produced a
similar creator layer. New infrastructure rewards both platform founders and
people who discover productive behavior on the platform.

Waiting for a polished playbook is costly. Once conventional courses teach the
workflow, early practitioners have accumulated skill, reputation, and distribution.

## Find the Community That Is Still Comparing Notes

Early knowledge is rarely organized. The hosts point to small online communities
where curious people share experiments before an industry has a name. Their
vocabulary, norms, and use cases are still unsettled.

Look for evidence of work rather than hype:

- people publishing artifacts, not only predictions;
- users combining tools in ways the vendor did not document;
- repeated complaints about the same technical limit;
- someone charging for a manual version of a new workflow;
- customers returning even though the experience is awkward.

The goal is not to imitate every experiment. It is to learn fast enough to see
which behavior persists as the technology improves.

## Original Example: One-Person Property Reports

A building inspector photographs each room and spends three hours turning notes
into a client report. New multimodal models can identify common fixtures,
organize images by room, and draft descriptions. The opportunity is not merely
an "AI report" button for a large inspection company.

A solo tradesperson may now complete twice as many reports without hiring an
administrator. A founder could test a workflow built around that newly viable
producer: capture evidence on site, require the inspector to approve every
finding, and deliver a branded report the same day. The removed constraint is
documentation labor. The product still must prove accuracy, trust, and repeated
willingness to pay.

## Try It Yourself: Build an Opportunity Map

Pick one capability released or substantially improved in the last year. Fill
in these five boxes:

1. **Before:** the old workflow, including time, headcount, and cash required.
2. **Shift:** the specific cost or capability that changed by at least an order
   of magnitude.
3. **New producer:** the person or small team that can now attempt the work.
4. **New behavior:** what they can do repeatedly that was previously impractical.
5. **Proof:** one transaction or weekly-use test you can run without building a
   full platform.

Then speak with three early practitioners. Ask what they have already made,
where the tool still fails, and what they currently pay to finish the job.

<details>
<summary>🧭 A wave does not remove customer discovery (optional)</summary>

A cheaper capability may be unreliable, regulated, or easy for competitors to
copy. Platform businesses also carry platform risk: fees, ranking, access, or
policies can change. Treat the technical shift as an opening for experiments,
not proof of demand or defensibility. The best early position is usually close
to real users and flexible about the final product shape.

</details>

## Three Things to Keep

- A major technology shift removes a concrete constraint and makes some previously uneconomic activity practical.
- Look for newly empowered producers and repeated new behaviors, not an old pitch decorated with a new feature.
- Learn alongside early practitioners, then test a transaction or habit before the market settles on a standard form.
