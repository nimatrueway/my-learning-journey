# Y Combinator Is Back In Person

Remote preserves access and focused work. Physical proximity can create trust,
weak ties, and side conversations that scheduled calls rarely reproduce. The
useful question is which interactions deserve each format.

⏱️ **~7 min** · includes 2 minutes of practice · the 20-minute source video is optional

## S22 Was Hybrid by Design

The episode announces that YC’s Summer 2022 batch would return to in-person
components. Founders would begin with a retreat in Sonoma and meet for an
end-of-batch celebration with alumni. Talks and office hours would remain remote.
This was not a blanket declaration that every useful interaction must happen in
one room. YC assigned different formats to different jobs.

Remote talks distribute information efficiently. Office hours can stay focused
without travel. A retreat does something else: founders meet repeatedly, develop
context, and encounter peers outside a fixed agenda. The hosts point to an
end-of-batch survey in which founders from the preceding batch were almost
unanimous about wanting more in-person components and said they would have moved
for them. That feedback changed the program.

The sequence matters too. A concentrated beginning can create relationships and
shared references that make later remote communication richer. A final gathering
can reconnect people whose work diverged during the batch. In-person time is
therefore not only a substitute for a video call. Used sparingly, it can change
the quality of the calls and messages that happen afterward.

## Name the Missing Outcome

“We need an office” is not an outcome. Neither is “remote is modern.” Diagnose
the work:

- information transfer may need a document or recorded talk;
- a disputed architecture may need a live working session;
- trust between new co-founders may need repeated unstructured time;
- customer discovery may require being where the work occurs;
- deep production may improve with fewer synchronous interruptions.

Choose format after naming the failure. Measure whether the chosen interaction
changed decision speed, relationship depth, recruiting, customer access, or
output. Attendance alone rewards events for existing.

## Ecosystem Density Has a Price

The hosts observe founders moving back toward large centers because they felt
lonely and wanted to be where startup activity was concentrated. They distinguish
joining an ecosystem from trying to build one alone. Density can make useful
people and information easier to encounter.

Relocation still consumes money, disrupts families, and can exclude people. A
startup whose customers and talent are distributed may gain little from a
permanent headquarters. Test density before making it identity: spend two weeks
near customers, run a monthly workday, or attend a domain event with explicit
meetings and follow-up.

## Original Example: The Hardware Integration Week

A remote robotics team loses days shipping prototypes between mechanical and
software engineers. It does not mandate a permanent office. Instead, the team
runs a quarterly integration week near the test site with one target: complete
the sensor-to-control loop on the same hardware.

Design reviews remain remote, and individual coding returns to uninterrupted
home schedules. The company measures defects resolved during the week and the
next quarter’s shipping delays. Co-location is a tool for a coupling problem,
not a moral judgment about remote work.

## Try It Yourself: Design a Format Experiment

Choose one recurring interaction that underperforms. Write its required outcome,
current format, and suspected failure. Run the opposite format once: turn a
status meeting into an async update, or turn a stuck remote debate into a
half-day working session. Beforehand, choose one observable result such as a
decision completed or handoff time reduced. Review cost and access as well.

<details>
<summary>🧭 Presence is not participation (optional)</summary>

An office can reproduce isolation through headphones and back-to-back calls.
Travel requirements can exclude caregivers, disabled teammates, and distributed
talent. Design the interaction and accommodations deliberately; proximity alone
does not create trust or useful work.

</details>

## Three Things to Keep

- YC’s S22 plan deliberately combined in-person relationship events with remote talks and office hours.
- Choose a format for an explicit information, coordination, relationship, or production outcome.
- Test ecosystem density and co-location against their financial, personal, and access costs before making a permanent move.
