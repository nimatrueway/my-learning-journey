# Part XVII: When Adult Life Feels Like an Endless Shift

You finish work, but the shift does not end. There is a repair to arrange,
something the baby needs, tomorrow's meeting, and a kitchen that has apparently
renewed its subscription to mess. You imagine retirement, not because you have
a detailed retirement plan, but because you want nobody to need anything for
a while.

⏱️ **~12 min** · includes 5 minutes of rehearsal and practice; folds are extra

That wish is worth understanding before judging. You can love your family,
value your home, and still feel depleted by the daily arrangement. Gratitude
and overload can coexist. Neither cancels the other.

## What does "I want to retire" mean today?

Try finishing the sentence: **"If I could stop tomorrow, the part I would most
want relief from is..."**

- **Interrupted sleep:** "I want to wake up rested."
- **Being on call:** "I want a period when someone else notices and handles things."
- **Lack of choice:** "I want some time that belongs to me."
- **Work itself:** "This job's demands or conditions are no longer sustainable."
- **Loss of enjoyment:** "Even free time does not feel good anymore."

These can overlap, but they suggest different next steps. A career change
cannot guarantee better sleep with a newborn. A nap cannot fix an abusive job.
A hobby block is not enough for persistent loss of enjoyment.

Treat retirement as a clue, not an instruction to resign and not proof that
you are failing adulthood.

## Why a baby can change the whole equation

A new baby can increase demands while reducing recovery, predictability, and
uninterrupted time. This matters for birth parents, fathers, and other
caregiving partners. A house adds recurring maintenance, financial commitments,
and decisions. The change is not merely "more tasks." It can be **less time
when you are genuinely off duty**.

There is also the work of remembering: noticing supplies, anticipating an
appointment, arranging help, and checking that something happened. If you must
assign and supervise every task, receiving help may not remove the management
load.

In this lesson, **role overload** means demands across your roles exceeding
the time and resources available. It is a useful description, not a diagnosis.
Do not automatically call all exhaustion burnout: the WHO's formal burnout
description concerns chronic workplace stress. Parenting strain and depression
are related questions, not interchangeable labels.

Compare that parent with someone who sleeps adequately but has no say over
their schedule. Their first useful change might be protected choice rather
than another sleep strategy. Matching the bottleneck matters.

## Why "every adult does this" is a harsh comparison

From outside, you see someone working, parenting, and building a business.
You usually cannot see their childcare, money, partner's labor, health,
work flexibility, temperament, sacrifices, or difficult days. Some genuinely
have more energy or enjoy their arrangement. Others are paying costs you
cannot see. You cannot infer equal capacity from similar job titles or ages.

"Adults have responsibilities" is true. "Therefore this exact workload should
feel easy, and I should add a business" does not follow. Constant enthusiasm
is not a requirement for adulthood.

Instead of asking why you cannot match somebody's visible output, ask:
**"Which resources make their arrangement possible, and which are actually
available here?"** If the answer is unknown, leave it unknown.

## Recover choice without abandoning care

Autonomy means experiencing some ownership of your actions, not having no
obligations. You might willingly choose parenthood while disliking parts of
the daily routine. Choosing a role does not mean consenting to every possible
distribution of its work.

Try three adjustments, starting with just one:

| Adjustment | Concrete example | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reduce a nonessential standard | Pause a cosmetic house project; simplify weekday meals | Keep safety, essential maintenance, nutrition, and care intact |
| Transfer full ownership | Someone owns a repair from booking through follow-up | Agree on scope; do not silently move overload to someone else |
| Protect reciprocal off-duty time | Each caregiver gets an agreed block without routine interruptions | Arrange safe coverage; emergencies still count |

If there is no partner or spare money, paid outsourcing may not be an option.
A trusted friend, family member, community resource, employer flexibility,
or parent-support service may help. Availability varies; a missing support
network is a real constraint, not a motivation problem.

Off-duty time need not produce anything. A walk, a friend, music, or sitting
quietly can count. Turning every recovered minute into self-improvement
recreates the shift you were trying to leave.

## Tiny example: change the week, not your whole identity

In this fictional example, Sam thinks, "I need to quit working forever."
After writing down the week, Sam notices that every evening includes both
childcare and coordinating a nonurgent house project.

Sam and their partner pause that project for a month, divide one recurring
chore by full ownership, and arrange a short off-duty block for each person.
They review whether the change helped after a week. Nothing proves Sam's job
is right long-term. The smaller experiment gives better information before
a major financial decision.

If rest improves things, that supports keeping the change; it does not rule
out a health problem. If it does not help, do not conclude you are ungrateful.
Reconsider workload, sleep, health, and support.

## When this is more than a scheduling problem

Depression can occur after a baby arrives in fathers and partners as well as
birth parents. Persistent low mood, hopelessness, loss of enjoyment, marked
irritability, or difficulty functioning deserve assessment, especially if you
cannot sleep even when there is an opportunity. You do not need to prove
depression before discussing those changes with a clinician.

Severe or persistent tiredness can also have physical contributors. Discuss
it with primary care rather than assuming it is laziness or diagnosing it
from this lesson. Seek help sooner if symptoms are severe; do not wait for
a two-week threshold.

Wanting a break is not the same as wanting to die. If the wish becomes not
wanting to be alive, or you fear you might harm yourself or the baby, seek
urgent support. If you cannot stay safe, use emergency services; in the US,
call or text **988** for crisis support. See
[Signals, Spirals, and the Right Tool](/courses/performance-foundations/part-11-signals-and-tools#know-the-escalation-lanes)
for the existing escalation guide.

## 🧪 Try it: a five-minute load conversation

Allow about three minutes to write and two to rehearse the request:

1. List three recurring demands, including who remembers and follows up.
2. Circle the main bottleneck: sleep, workload, choice, support, or persistent
   symptoms. More than one may matter.
3. Choose one safe demand to simplify, defer, or transfer this week.
4. Rehearse: "I care about our life, and this arrangement is exhausting me.
   Can we change ___ for one week and protect rest for both of us?"
5. Set a review date. Look for changes in rest, irritability, enjoyment, and
   ability to function, not whether you became an energetic entrepreneur.

The badge's five-minute allowance covers both the earlier scenario and this
short practice; implementing the agreement takes additional time.

<details>
<summary>🧠 Motivation without pretending duties are fun (optional)</summary>

Self-determination theory distinguishes motivation experienced as chosen or
personally endorsed from motivation driven mainly by pressure, guilt, or
approval. Its framework emphasizes autonomy, competence, and relatedness.
It does not predict that endorsing a responsibility makes unlimited demands
sustainable.

The practical load map and one-week experiment here are teaching heuristics,
not validated screening tools or a treatment protocol. Sleep disruption,
relationship conditions, financial constraints, physical health, and mental
health can interact. A useful model should help investigate, not close the
case prematurely.

</details>

## 🐾 Three things to keep

- Wanting escape can be information about load, recovery, or lost choice; it
  is not a verdict on your love or maturity.
- Change one part of the arrangement, including invisible management work,
  before demanding a better attitude or adding another ambition.
- Persistent loss of enjoyment, impaired functioning, or severe exhaustion
  calls for assessment, not only a more efficient calendar.
