# Module 7 — Audio & Video Models 🎧🎬

You already know how networks learn from images and sequences. Now give one a recording of “feed the cat” and a video of a door opening. What survives the trip from a media file to a tensor? What gets lost before the model even runs?

<Meme top="The cat applied for both sound engineer and film editor." visual="🐱🎧🎬" bottom="First assignment: stop treating every input as a JPEG." />

The through-line is **evidence → representation → prediction → test**. Audio needs a correct clock and useful frequency features. Video needs useful moments in the right order. Neither a fluent transcript nor a confident label proves that the pipeline is correct.

⏱️ **~52 min total** · reading, interactive practice, and lab; optional folds, installation, and model downloads are extra.

## Lessons

1. [Audio models: from waveforms to words](/courses/deep-learning/module-7/lesson-1-audio-models) — ~10 min. Trace a waveform into features, distinguish patches from words, and count a transcription error.
2. [Video models: teaching an image model about time](/courses/deep-learning/module-7/lesson-2-video-models) — ~11 min. Reverse a door-opening sequence, budget tokens, and identify what sparse sampling hides.
3. [🛠 Mini-project: run pretrained audio and video models](/courses/deep-learning/module-7/mini-project) — ~31 min. Load your own media and compare normal, reversed, and frozen clips without overclaiming what a score proves.

## Bring what you already know

You need the [image and convolution intuition from Module 4](/courses/deep-learning/module-4/) and the [attention intuition from Module 5](/courses/deep-learning/module-5/). No signal-processing background is assumed. The lessons' widgets and paper exercises need only a browser; the project needs Python and short recordings you can use with permission.

Whisper appears here as a transcription tool; [Module 8](/courses/deep-learning/module-8/) opens it up and explains the encoder–decoder shape that makes it work.
