A title is usually more focused than a body. But copying the title into three
other fields should not automatically make an article three times more useful.

⏱️ **Reading + practice: ~9 min** · folds are optional extra.

<Meme top="The cat copied one fact into three fields." visual="😼📚" bottom="Three mentions are not automatically three independent reasons. Choose sum versus DisMax deliberately." />

## Decide how evidence combines

Searching title and body requires two decisions: which fields may match, and
how their score contributions combine. A Boolean query with `SHOULD` clauses
can sum matching contributions. `DisjunctionMaxQuery` instead uses the highest
subquery score plus a tie-breaker fraction of the other matching scores. A tie
parameter of zero takes only the best; a parameter of one yields the sum.

Neither choice is universally correct. Summation rewards agreement across
independent fields, but duplicate evidence can dominate. Best-field behavior is
often useful when title, summary, and body represent overlapping descriptions.
The scores themselves still depend on field-specific term statistics and norms;
a multiplier is not a portable probability of relevance.

## Run the title/body comparison

Run `FullTextLab multifield` using [the advanced setup](../advanced-labs.mdx).
It returns A before B for `bicycle` using:

```java
Query query = new DisjunctionMaxQuery(List.of(
    new BoostQuery(new TermQuery(new Term("title", "bicycle")), 3),
    new TermQuery(new Term("body", "bicycle"))), 0.1f);
```

A's title is `Bicycle repairs`; B's title is `Travel guide`. Both bodies match,
but A also has the stronger title signal. The lab wraps the query with the same
public-access filter as other feature modes. A perfect private title is still
ineligible. A boost never substitutes for authorization.

This fragment uses already normalized exact terms. For a real text input,
`MultiFieldQueryParser` can analyze clauses across configured fields with boosts,
or you can use `QueryBuilder` and assemble the field queries yourself. Explicit
construction gives you control over best-field versus summed evidence and
required clauses. Inspect what the parser actually builds before assigning a
product meaning to its defaults.

## Rehearse duplicated evidence

Imagine the same title copied into title, summary, and tags. Choose how to
combine their scores. These are qualitative outcomes; run your judged queries
before choosing a tie-breaker.

## Analysis and field boundaries

Keep exact identifiers in exact fields and prose in analyzed fields. A title
analyzer may differ from body analysis, but every query branch must produce
compatible terms for its target. A phrase cannot magically cross title and body
simply because both are part of the document. If a combined field is needed,
define it at ingestion and understand its length and positional semantics.

Multiple values of one text field also raise position-gap questions. Values are
not automatically separate nested objects. Decide whether a phrase may span
values and configure the analyzer's position increment gap appropriately. For
related attributes such as color and size, a gap does not solve tuple integrity;
the block-join lesson addresses that different problem.

## Try it yourself 🧪

Change the tie-breaker from 0.1 to zero and then one. Print `searcher.explain`
for A and B from the same reader and locate the best-field and secondary terms.
Next copy `bicycle` into B's title and compare again. Do not demand that a
specific boost always gives a fixed score margin after changing corpus statistics.

Write six intent-specific judgments: three title-oriented lookups and three
questions answered only in the body. Tune on four and hold two aside. If title
boosting helps navigational queries but suppresses detailed answers, report the
tradeoff by query type instead of hiding it in one average.

they are gates, sort keys, or scoring features. A legal publication date filter
is not equivalent to a freshness boost. Multiplying text relevance by unbounded
popularity can bury new content. Calibrate features with judgments, retain a
lexical baseline, and inspect failure cases before reaching for a learned ranker.

## Recap 🐾

1. Multi-field matching and field-score combination are separate choices.
2. DisjunctionMaxQuery limits secondary contributions; boosts do not enforce eligibility.
3. Field analysis, phrase boundaries, and query intent determine whether a ranking rule is useful.
