# 🇨🇭 Switzerland: Proverbs and Sayings

⏱️ **~6 min** · read once, revisit often

Can you keep both the coin and the bread roll you bought with it? A Swiss
German saying makes a difficult tradeoff sound like a trip to the bakery.
That is a useful starting point: ordinary objects can carry quite sharp advice.

Switzerland has four national languages: German, French, Italian, and Romansh.
This selection includes **16 Swiss German sayings**, followed by **four shared
French and Italian proverbs**. It does not yet cover Romansh or every regional
tradition. The French and Italian entries belong to wider language communities,
not exclusively to Switzerland.

Swiss German has many dialects and no single everyday spelling standard.
The forms below preserve variants documented in the linked collection rather
than pretending to be one standardized Swiss language. Meanings and examples
are original explanations; these sayings are cultural material, not evidence
that everyone in Switzerland thinks alike.

## Tradeoffs, money, and effort

1. **Chasch nöd de Feufer und s Weggli ha.** · Swiss German, Zurich variant

   *Literally:* "You can't have the five-cent coin and the bread roll."
   Spending something means giving it up. Like "you can't have your cake and
   eat it," this is useful when someone wants incompatible benefits. Other
   dialects name a different coin.

2. **Vo nüt chund nüt.** · Swiss German

   *Literally:* "From nothing comes nothing." Results need effort or resources.
   It is shared with the wider German-speaking tradition, rather than a uniquely
   Swiss claim about work.

3. **Hütt än Rappe, morn än Rappe, gits ä schuöni Zipfelchapä.** · Swiss German

   *Literally:* "A cent today, a cent tomorrow, makes a nice pointed cap."
   Small savings eventually buy something. Compare the Swedish
   [small streams becoming a river](./06-sweden.md#small-steps-and-common-sense).

4. **Vo de Riiche muesch s Spare lerne.** · Swiss German

   *Literally:* "You must learn saving from the rich." Wealth does not
   necessarily mean careless spending. It can be offered sincerely or with a
   pointed smile; it does not explain how any particular fortune was made.

## Compromise, questions, and perspective

5. **De Gschider git na, de Esel blibt sta.** · Swiss German, Zurich variant

   *Literally:* "The wiser one gives way; the donkey stays standing."
   Refusing to budge is not the same as being right. A compromise may be wiser
   than winning a small argument.

6. **Frage chostet nüt.** · Swiss German

   *Literally:* "Asking costs nothing." Ask about the vacancy, the discount, or
   the unfamiliar rule instead of assuming the answer.

7. **Froge darf me, wenn me d’Antwort nid schücht.** · Swiss German

   *Literally:* "You may ask if you do not fear the answer." The counterweight
   to the previous saying: inviting honesty means accepting an uncomfortable reply.

8. **Jedes Problemli het zwöi siite: die fauschi ond üsi.** · Swiss German

   *Literally:* "Every little problem has two sides: the wrong one and ours."
   A joke about our ability to mistake our own position for objective truth.
   Read it as self-mockery, not advice to dismiss disagreement.

## Patience and incomplete evidence

9. **Bis denn fliest no vill Wasser de Rhy derab.** · Swiss German, Basel variant

   *Literally:* "A lot of water will flow down the Rhine before then."
   Something is still a long way off. Regional versions use the Limmat or the
   Rhône instead: the landscape changes, but the advice travels.

10. **E Schwalbe macht no ke Summer.** · Swiss German

    *Literally:* "One swallow does not make a summer." A single promising sign
    is not enough to establish a lasting change.

11. **Das isch weder Fisch no Vogel.** · Swiss German

    *Literally:* "That is neither fish nor bird." Something fits neither
    category, often because a compromise has left it without a clear purpose.

12. **Dä dümmschdi Buur hät die gröschdä Härdöpfel!** · Swiss German

    *Literally:* "The stupidest farmer has the biggest potatoes."
    Success does not always track skill. The saying can express envy as much
    as insight, so it is not a fair assessment of an actual farmer.

## Courtesy, eccentricity, and consequences

13. **Jedem Lappi siis Chappi.** · Swiss German

    *Literally:* "To every fool their cap." Let people have their peculiar
    preferences. It can sound affectionate or dismissive, depending on tone.

14. **So luut wieme i Waud mööget, chunnts ou zrügg.** · Swiss German

    *Literally:* "As loudly as you shout into the forest, it comes back."
    Your manner shapes the response you receive. It shares its echo image
    with German versions of the same saying.

15. **Wenn’s em Esel z’wohl wird gaht er ufs Iis.** · Swiss German

    *Literally:* "When the donkey gets too comfortable, it goes onto the ice."
    Security can tempt someone into unnecessary risks. Comfort is not itself
    a problem; forgetting the danger is.

16. **S’git nüt wo’s nit git.** · Swiss German

    *Literally:* "There is nothing that doesn't exist." An amused response to
    something astonishing: people and circumstances can still surprise you.

## Shared French and Italian traditions

These entries illustrate the languages of Romandie and Italian-speaking
Switzerland. Their wording is standard French or Italian, not a claim of
Swiss origin or a survey of which sayings are most frequent locally.

17. **Petit à petit, l'oiseau fait son nid.** · French

    *Literally:* "Little by little, the bird makes its nest." Patient,
    repeated contributions build something lasting.

18. **L'union fait la force.** · French

    *Literally:* "Unity makes strength." Cooperation can achieve what isolated
    effort cannot. The phrase is used beyond Switzerland, including as a
    motto elsewhere; it is not the Swiss national motto.

19. **Chi va piano va sano e va lontano.** · Italian

    *Literally:* "Who goes slowly goes safely and goes far." A sustainable
    pace can beat a hurried start.

20. **Tra il dire e il fare c'è di mezzo il mare.** · Italian

    *Literally:* "Between saying and doing lies the sea." Promising a result
    is much easier than delivering it. The sea is part of the shared Italian
    image, not an illustration of Swiss geography.

## Read the sayings against each other

Yielding can be wise, but a muddled compromise can become "neither fish nor
bird." Asking costs nothing, yet the answer may be hard to hear. These tensions
make the collection more useful than a tidy list of supposed national traits.
Choose the saying that illuminates a situation, not the one that stereotypes
the person in front of you.
