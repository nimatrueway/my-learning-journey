# 🇸🇪 Sweden: Proverbs and Sayings

⏱️ **~4 min** · read once, revisit often

Swedish **ordspråk** (proverbs) and **talesätt** (sayings) come from a land of
long winters, scattered farms, and dense forests. Many are shared, almost word
for word, with Norwegian and Danish, because the three languages are closely
related and partly mutually understandable.

Each entry gives the Swedish, a literal translation, and the usual meaning.
Notes describe common readings, not a fixed national character.

## Lagom and modesty

1. **Lagom är bäst.**

   *Literally:* "Just the right amount is best." *Lagom* means "not too much,
   not too little." It has no exact English translation and is often presented
   as the key to Swedish taste: moderate consumption, balanced work, understated
   design.

2. **Själv är bäste dräng.**

   *Literally:* "Oneself is the best farmhand." If you want it done right, do
   it yourself. Self-reliance from a society of isolated farms.

3. **Tala är silver, tiga är guld.**

   *Literally:* "Speaking is silver, silence is gold." Swedes are often
   comfortable with silence in conversation.

4. **Bättre fly än illa fäkta.**

   *Literally:* "Better to flee than to fight badly." Avoid a fight you can't
   win, a cautious practicality that some link to Sweden's long avoidance of war
   since 1814.

## Weather and the outdoors

5. **Det finns inget dåligt väder, bara dåliga kläder.**

   *Literally:* "There's no bad weather, only bad clothes." Shared across
   Scandinavia. Children play outside in rain and snow, and many preschools
   spend much of the day outdoors.

6. **Efter regn kommer solsken.**

   *Literally:* "After rain comes sunshine." Bad times pass.

7. **Lugnet före stormen**

   *Literally:* "The calm before the storm."

## Patience and caution

8. **Den som väntar på något gott väntar aldrig för länge.**

   *Literally:* "Whoever waits for something good never waits too long." Swedes
   are known for orderly queuing, often with a numbered ticket (*kölapp*).

9. **Man ska inte sälja skinnet förrän björnen är skjuten.**

   *Literally:* "Don't sell the hide before the bear is shot." Don't count your
   chickens before they hatch, in a hunter's version.

10. **Man ska inte ropa hej förrän man är över bäcken.**

    *Literally:* "Don't shout hooray until you're over the stream." Don't
    celebrate too early.

11. **Liten tuva stjälper ofta stora lass.**

    *Literally:* "A small tussock often overturns a big load." Small problems
    can cause big trouble.

12. **Gå som katten kring het gröt**

    *Literally:* "To walk like the cat around hot porridge." To avoid the main
    issue, beating around the bush. Norwegian and Danish share it.

## Small steps and common sense

13. **Många bäckar små gör en stor å.**

    *Literally:* "Many small streams make a big river." Little contributions
    add up.

14. **Man ska inte gå över ån efter vatten.**

    *Literally:* "Don't cross the river to fetch water." Don't make things
    harder than necessary; use what's close at hand.

15. **Ju fler kockar, desto sämre soppa.**

    *Literally:* "The more cooks, the worse the soup."

16. **Bättre sent än aldrig.**

    *Literally:* "Better late than never."

17. **Den som gräver en grop åt andra faller själv däri.**

    *Literally:* "Whoever digs a pit for others falls in it themselves."

## Home and togetherness

18. **Borta bra men hemma bäst.**

    *Literally:* "Away is good, but home is best." Norway and Denmark share this
    too.

19. **Delad glädje är dubbel glädje, delad sorg är halv sorg.**

    *Literally:* "Shared joy is double joy; shared sorrow is half sorrow."

20. **Smaken är som baken, delad.**

    *Literally:* "Taste is like the backside: divided." A cheeky way to say
    tastes differ. Everyone has their own.

21. **Ingen ko på isen.**

    *Literally:* "No cow on the ice." No need to worry. The full form adds
    *så länge rumpan är på land*, "as long as its rear is on land." Things may
    look risky, but they are under control.

## Humor and resilience

22. **Alla vill till himlen men ingen vill dö.**

    *Literally:* "Everyone wants to go to heaven, but nobody wants to die."
    Everyone wants the reward without the cost.

23. **Ont krut förgås inte så lätt.**

    *Literally:* "Bad gunpowder doesn't perish easily." Troublemakers, or tough
    people, are hard to get rid of. Often said fondly about a hardy relative.

## What the collection suggests

Swedish sayings prize **balance** (lagom), **calm patience** (wait for good
things, don't sell the bear's hide), and **practical self-reliance** (be your
own farmhand, dress for the weather). The tone is mild and understated, with
humor that pokes rather than punches.

<details>
<summary>🤓 Lagom, fika, and allemansrätten (optional)</summary>

*Lagom* is sometimes said to come from Viking drinking-horn etiquette, "laget
om," passed "around the team," so each person took only their share. Linguists
consider this folk etymology; the word more likely comes from an old form
meaning "according to law" or custom.

**Fika** is a coffee-and-pastry break, often built into the workday. It is less
a snack than a social ritual for pausing together.

**Allemansrätten**, the "right of public access," lets anyone walk, camp, and
pick berries on most land, provided they respect nature and landowners. It
explains why weather proverbs matter: Swedes expect to be outdoors.

</details>
