# 🇮🇳 India: Proverbs and Sayings

⏱️ **~6 min** · read once, revisit often

Someone cannot dance, so they blame the crooked courtyard. Another person
underestimates the food at home while admiring a distant feast. The images
are different; both ask whether the problem is in the world or in our judgment.

India does not have a single proverb language. This initial collection gives
**12 Hindi sayings and eight Tamil sayings**, with each language identified.
It is not an exhaustive or representative survey of India's many linguistic,
regional, religious, and social traditions. Bengali, Marathi, Punjabi, Telugu,
Kannada, Malayalam, Gujarati, and other traditions are not covered here yet.

The Hindi entries include expressions shared with Urdu and other South Asian
languages; Tamil proverbs also circulate outside India. A national heading is
a navigation aid, not a claim that a saying belongs exclusively to that country.
Romanizations are reading aids rather than exact pronunciation instructions.
Literal translations and interpretations below are original renderings.

## Hindi: skill, proof, and responsibility

1. **नाच न जाने, आँगन टेढ़ा।** · *nāch na jāne, āṅgan ṭeṛhā*

   *Literally:* "Doesn't know how to dance; the courtyard is crooked."
   Someone blames their surroundings for their own lack of skill.
   Like "a bad workman blames his tools," the proverb challenges an excuse,
   but it cannot establish whether an actual obstacle is real.

2. **हाथ कंगन को आरसी क्या।** · *hāth kaṅgan ko ārsī kyā*

   *Literally:* "Why a mirror for the bracelet on your wrist?"
   What is plainly visible needs no further demonstration. *Ārsī* refers
   to a mirror; the image contrasts direct evidence with unnecessary proof.

3. **उल्टा चोर कोतवाल को डाँटे।** · *ulṭā chor kotvāl ko ḍāṅṭe*

   *Literally:* "Instead, the thief scolds the police officer."
   The wrongdoer turns accuser. It points to blame being reversed rather
   than simply two people sharing the same fault.

4. **अब पछताए होत क्या जब चिड़िया चुग गई खेत।** · *ab pachhtāe hot kyā jab chiṛiyā chug gaī khet*

   *Literally:* "What use is regret now, when the birds have eaten the field?"
   The opportunity to prevent the damage has passed. The agricultural image
   makes the cost of acting too late concrete.

## Hindi: distance, familiarity, and appreciation

5. **दूर के ढोल सुहावने लगते हैं।** · *dūr ke ḍhol suhāvne lagte haiṅ*

   *Literally:* "Distant drums sound pleasant." A distant life or opportunity
   looks attractive when its difficulties are out of earshot.
   Compare Korea's [larger-looking rice cake](./02-korea.md#envy-comparison-and-fortune).

6. **घर की मुर्गी दाल बराबर।** · *ghar kī murgī dāl barābar*

   *Literally:* "The chicken at home is equal to lentils."
   Familiar advantages get taken for granted. The comparison depends on
   treating chicken as a special dish and lentils as ordinary food; that
   is the proverb's setup, not a hierarchy everyone shares.

7. **बंदर क्या जाने अदरक का स्वाद।** · *bandar kyā jāne adrak kā svād*

   *Literally:* "What does a monkey know of ginger's taste?"
   Something is offered to someone who cannot appreciate it. Like
   [Japan's gold coins for a cat](./03-japan.md#health-and-the-famous-cats),
   it can sound insulting when aimed at another person.

8. **जंगल में मोर नाचा, किसने देखा?** · *jaṅgal meṅ mor nāchā, kisne dekhā?*

   *Literally:* "The peacock danced in the forest; who saw it?"
   Talent or achievement may pass unnoticed without an audience.
   It asks about recognition, not whether unseen work has no value.

## Hindi: power, belonging, and survival

9. **जिसकी लाठी उसकी भैंस।** · *jiskī lāṭhī uskī bhaiṅs*

   *Literally:* "Whoever has the stick has the buffalo."
   Power can determine possession instead of justice. This is often a
   criticism of coercion, not an endorsement of "might makes right."

10. **घर का भेदी लंका ढाए।** · *ghar kā bhedī laṅkā ḍhāe*

    *Literally:* "An insider brings down Lanka." Someone who knows a group's
    secrets can undo it. The image invokes the Ramayana's Lanka; it is a
    story reference, not a claim about present-day Sri Lanka.

11. **जल में रहकर मगर से बैर ठीक नहीं।** · *jal meṅ rahkar magar se bair ṭhīk nahīṅ*

    *Literally:* "Living in water, enmity with the crocodile is not wise."
    Dependence on someone powerful can make confrontation risky.
    Describing that constraint does not make their abuse acceptable.

12. **जान है तो जहान है।** · *jān hai to jahān hai*

    *Literally:* "If there is life, there is a world."
    Staying alive preserves future possibilities. It can reset priorities
    when a possession, contest, or immediate gain is being treated as everything.

## Tamil: learning, ingenuity, and humility

13. **கற்றது கை மண் அளவு; கல்லாதது உலகளவு.** · *kaṟṟatu kai maṇ aḷavu; kallātatu ulakaḷavu*

    *Literally:* "What is learned is a handful of sand; what is unlearned
    is the size of the world." Knowledge should increase humility, not
    the illusion that learning is finished.

14. **வல்லவனுக்கு புல்லும் ஆயுதம்.** · *vallavaṉukku pullum āyutam*

    *Literally:* "For a capable person, even grass is a weapon."
    Ingenuity makes simple resources useful. The weapon is the proverb's
    image; its everyday application need not involve fighting.

15. **வல்லவனுக்கு வல்லவன் வையகத்தில் உண்டு.** · *vallavaṉukku vallavaṉ vaiyakattil uṇṭu*

    *Literally:* "For a capable person, there is another capable person
    in the world." There is always someone who can match or surpass you.
    Confidence is useful; assuming no equal is not.

16. **அகத்தின் அழகு முகத்தில் தெரியும்.** · *akattiṉ aḻaku mukattil teriyum*

    *Literally:* "Inner beauty shows in the face." A person's feelings or
    character may show in their expression. Treat this as an observation
    about expression, not a reliable way to infer morality from appearance.

## Tamil: small efforts, cooperation, and limits

17. **சிறு துளி பெரு வெள்ளம்.** · *ciṟu tuḷi peru veḷḷam*

    *Literally:* "Small drop, great flood." Small contributions accumulate.
    Its water image is a useful companion to the Nordic small streams and
    Chinese dust or patient-effort images elsewhere in this collection.

18. **ஒற்றுமையே வலிமை.** · *oṟṟumaiyē valimai*

    *Literally:* "Unity itself is strength." People working together can
    achieve more than isolated individuals. It is an ideal of cooperation,
    not proof that any society is free from internal conflict.

19. **காற்றுள்ளபோதே தூற்றிக் கொள்.** · *kāṟṟuḷḷapōtē tūṟṟik koḷ*

    *Literally:* "Winnow while there is wind." Use favorable conditions while
    they last. Winnowing separates grain from chaff with moving air:
    the practical task makes the advice immediately understandable.

20. **அளவுக்கு மிஞ்சினால் அமிர்தமும் நஞ்சு.** · *aḷavukku miñciṉāl amirtamum nañcu*

    *Literally:* "If it exceeds the limit, even nectar is poison."
    Something beneficial can become harmful in excess. *Amirtam* evokes
    the nectar of immortality, making the warning about excess especially strong.

## Compare images, not national personalities

The crooked courtyard questions an excuse; the stick and buffalo question
power. The handful of sand urges humility, while grass becoming a useful tool
urges confidence in skill. There is no single attitude that reconciles every
entry, and no reason to expect one. These are ways to frame a situation,
sometimes wisely and sometimes unfairly.
