# 🇩🇰 Denmark: Proverbs and Sayings

⏱️ **~4 min** · read once, revisit often

Danish **ordsprog** (proverbs) and **talemåder** (expressions) are known for
dry, understated humor. Many are shared with Norwegian, since the two countries
were united for more than four centuries and Danish was Norway's written
language for much of that time.

Each entry gives the Danish, a literal translation, and the usual meaning.
Notes describe common readings, not a fixed national character.

## Equality and Janteloven

1. **Du skal ikke tro, du er noget.**

   *Literally:* "You shall not think you are anything." The first rule of
   **Janteloven**. Its author, Aksel Sandemose, was born in Denmark and modeled
   the fictional town of Jante on his hometown, Nykøbing Mors, before moving to
   Norway and writing the novel in Norwegian. Danes debate whether the attitude
   keeps society fair or holds ambition back.

2. **Lige børn leger bedst.**

   *Literally:* "Similar children play best." Birds of a feather flock
   together; people get along best with their own kind.

3. **Man skal ikke skue hunden på hårene.**

   *Literally:* "Don't judge the dog by its hair." Don't judge by appearances.

## Unity and community

4. **Enighed gør stærk.**

   *Literally:* "Unity makes strong." A favorite saying in a small country
   that relies on consensus, trust, and collective welfare.

5. **Mange bække små gør en stor å.**

   *Literally:* "Many small brooks make a big river." Shared with Swedish and
   Norwegian.

6. **Ude godt, men hjemme bedst.**

   *Literally:* "Out is good, but home is best." Home is linked to **hygge**,
   the cozy warmth of candles, good food, and relaxed company. Danes are often
   reported to burn more candles per person than any other Europeans.

7. **Gammel kærlighed ruster ikke.**

   *Literally:* "Old love does not rust."

## Practical common sense

8. **Man skal ikke gå over åen efter vand.**

   *Literally:* "Don't cross the stream to fetch water." Don't look far away
   for what's right beside you.

9. **Hvad man ikke har i hovedet, må man have i benene.**

   *Literally:* "What you don't have in your head, you must have in your legs."
   If you forget something, you'll have to walk back for it.

10. **Man kan ikke både blæse og have mel i munden.**

    *Literally:* "You can't both blow and have flour in your mouth." You can't
    do two incompatible things at once; you can't have it both ways.

11. **Bedre en fugl i hånden end ti på taget.**

    *Literally:* "Better one bird in the hand than ten on the roof." The Danish
    version of "a bird in the hand is worth two in the bush" is ten times more
    cautious.

12. **Man skal ikke sælge skindet, før bjørnen er skudt.**

    *Literally:* "Don't sell the hide before the bear is shot."

13. **Det er nemt at være bagklog.**

    *Literally:* "It's easy to be back-wise." Hindsight is easy.

## Words, silence, and children

14. **Tale er sølv, tavshed er guld.**

    *Literally:* "Speech is silver, silence is gold."

15. **Små gryder har også ører.**

    *Literally:* "Small pots have ears too." Watch what you say; children are
    listening.

16. **Når katten er ude, spiller musene på bordet.**

    *Literally:* "When the cat is out, the mice play on the table."

## Courage, work, and finishing

17. **Hvo intet vover, intet vinder.**

    *Literally:* "Who dares nothing wins nothing."

18. **Morgenstund har guld i mund.**

    *Literally:* "The morning hour has gold in its mouth."

19. **Den, der ler sidst, ler bedst.**

    *Literally:* "The one who laughs last laughs best."

20. **Alle gode gange tre.**

    *Literally:* "All good times are three." Third time's the charm.

21. **Så er den ged barberet.**

    *Literally:* "Then that goat is shaved." Job done, task finished. Said with
    satisfaction after completing something tedious.

22. **Der er ikke noget, der hedder dårligt vejr, kun dårligt tøj.**

    *Literally:* "There's no such thing as bad weather, only bad clothes." Danes
    commute by bicycle year-round, so this is lived advice, especially in
    Copenhagen.

## What the collection suggests

Danish sayings combine **egalitarian modesty** (Janteloven, similar children
play best), **trust in community** (unity makes strong), and a love of
**cozy, unpretentious comfort** (home is best, hygge). The humor is dry and
domestic: shaved goats, flour in the mouth, and mice dancing on the table.

<details>
<summary>🤓 Hygge, trust, and caveats (optional)</summary>

**Hygge** became an international lifestyle trend around 2016. Inside Denmark it
is less a decorating style than a social mood: relaxed, equal, unhurried
company. Some Danes find the export version, with expensive candles and
blankets, a little amusing.

Denmark regularly ranks near the top of international surveys of happiness and
social trust. Researchers link these results to welfare institutions, low
corruption, and equality; proverbs like *enighed gør stærk* reflect those
values but do not explain them by themselves.

Sandemose's Janteloven is a literary satire. It is widely quoted in Denmark and
Norway, but it was not a real code of conduct.

</details>
