# DOs and DON'Ts

A running list of lessons, newest first within each section.

<WriterVaultProvider>

## ✅ DOs

<NoteEntry kind="do" date="2026-10-30" title="Aim to be the best, not merely good">

Set your sights on the best work in your field. Ambition tends to miss by
falling short, aiming to be the best may demand a qualitatively different kind
of commitment, and "good" is too vague to guide the work. A precise, demanding
standard gives your taste and effort something real to converge on.

Source: [Paul Graham, *How to Do Great Work*](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html)

</NoteEntry>

<NoteEntry kind="do" date="2026-10-30" title="Make it worthy before you test it">

Take a design to the point where you love it and are proud to put your name on
it before asking other people to judge it. Simplify by understanding the
problem's physics and first principles deeply enough to distill the solution to
its essence, then obsess over the details. Inside an organization, translate
that craft into its language: explain which real goal the work advances and
why customers loving the product benefits the business.

Source: [Brian Chesky, *Stanford Graduate School of Business interview*](https://youtu.be/Dkfijg7s76o?t=984)

</NoteEntry>

<NoteEntry kind="do" date="2026-10-21" title="Follow curiosity into ambitious work">

Choose work that fits both your natural aptitude and your deep interests, then
build a habit of pursuing your own excitingly ambitious projects. Work hard
without burning out, study the best examples, seek excellent colleagues, and
look closely for gaps at the frontier of what is known. Take affordable risks,
stay honest with yourself, and protect the health that lets you keep going.
When the path is unclear, follow your curiosity.

Source: [Paul Graham, *Superlinear Returns*](https://paulgraham.com/superlinear.html)

</NoteEntry>

<NoteEntry kind="do" date="2026-10-21" title="Choose work that compounds">

Look for work where today's progress makes tomorrow's progress easier.
Learning compounds because each new idea helps you absorb the next one;
audiences compound because existing fans bring new ones. The curve may feel
flat at first, so pay attention to the growth mechanism before the visible
results become dramatic.

Source: [Paul Graham, *How to Do Great Work*](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html)

</NoteEntry>

<NoteEntry kind="do" date="2026-10-21" title="Find work worth unreasonable attention">

Great work often requires spending far longer on a problem than other people
would consider reasonable. Choose work engaging enough that the time does not
feel merely like a cost. Genuine interest makes sustained attention possible;
without it, the price will eventually feel too high.

Source: [Paul Graham, *How to Do Great Work*](https://paulgraham.com/greatwork.html)

</NoteEntry>

<NoteEntry kind="do" date="2026-10-14" title="When avoiding work, name the feeling, then start">

When you catch yourself avoiding a chore or work task, write down the feelings
making you hesitate. Naming them can clarify what is getting in the way and
take some of the edge off. If the task still needs doing, spend just 10
minutes on it, then reassess how you feel. Starting can make continuing
easier; you do not always need to feel motivated first. And watch the classic
trap: avoidance wearing a productive disguise, like
[building tools instead of doing the work](/courses/performance-foundations/part-9-from-learning-to-doing#5-why-am-i-building-productivity-tools-instead-of-doing-my-work)
(🧠 Psychology, Part IX).

## 🚫 DON'Ts

<NoteEntry kind="dont" date="2026-10-30" title="Use testing or growth as a substitute for judgment">

Do not test a design before you are happy with it and expect other people to
finish the designer's thinking. Exercise your own taste and craft first. And do
not call growth the goal: growth is a direction. Keep asking what outcome the
growth is meant to serve, then connect the design to that outcome.

Source: [Brian Chesky, *Stanford Graduate School of Business interview*](https://youtu.be/Dkfijg7s76o?t=984)

</NoteEntry>

<NoteEntry kind="dont" date="2026-10-14" title="Let the list go stale">

Do not let this list go stale. Keep adding lessons as you learn them and
revisit entries when your experience changes.

</NoteEntry>
